Here is a list of verbal tics:  

<A> Admission of fault/bias: Narrator directly states that he/she has made mistakes, has particular biases, does not know all the details, or is reporting information from another character who is likely unreliable. Examples: "Like others of my generation...", "I tend to see things from a particular point of view"    
<B> Defensive tone: Narrator uses multiple phrases in protestation. Examples: "Let me make perfectly clear", "I should say", "I should point out", "let me make it immediately clear", "I feel I should explain"
<C> Digressions: Narrator veers off-topic at least once. Examples: "I will do that in a minute. By the way,..."
<D> Hedging language: Narrator uses phrases that indicate uncertainty or vagueness. Examples: "it seems that", "it appears to be", "I think", "maybe", "sort of"
<E> Inconsistencies: Narrator gives contradicting statements and/or events don't add up. "I am a nobody. But look! There is a plane drawing my name in the sky."  
<F> Selective Memory: Narrator acknowledges they may have forgotten important details or their memory is faulty. Examples: "It was so long ago, it's hard to remember", "My memory is not what it used to be"
<G> Statement of Potential Disbelief: Narrator acknowledges the narrative may sound unlikely. Examples: "You might not believe me, but", "what happened next might seem strange"
